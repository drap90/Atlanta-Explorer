Hey, I'm glad you're here. Atlanta doesn't hand itself over in one postcard. It's a dogwood-shaded, hip-hop-scored, sweet-tea-and-civil-rights kind of place, spread across neighborhoods that each feel like their own small town. This is the weekend I'd plan for a friend.

01

Landmarks

Four places that explain the city better than any brochure.

Sweet Auburn · History

Martin Luther King Jr. National Historical Park

Dr. King's birth home, Ebenezer Baptist Church, and his resting place sit within a few blocks on Auburn Avenue. Stand in the sanctuary where he preached, then sit by the reflecting pool. Visitor center entry is free.

Local move: walk Auburn Avenue afterward and look for the murals honoring Congressman John Lewis.

Old Fourth Ward · Outdoors

The BeltLine Eastside Trail

An old rail corridor turned into the city's front porch: two-plus miles of murals, patios, dogs, and skaters linking Piedmont Park to Krog Street Tunnel and Ponce City Market.

Midtown · Park

Piedmont Park

Two hundred acres of lawn where the city stretches out on a Saturday. The view of Midtown over Lake Clara Meer is the one locals use for their holiday cards.

Downtown · 1996 Olympics

Centennial Olympic Park

Built for the 1996 Summer Games, now the hub for the Georgia Aquarium, World of Coca-Cola, and the National Center for Civil and Human Rights. Kids run through the Fountain of Rings on summer evenings.

Local move: the Center for Civil and Human Rights is the one I'd never skip. Give it two hours.

02

Local Food

Three dishes. Bring an appetite and some napkins.

  1. No. 1

    Lemon pepper wings

    Atlanta's unofficial city dish: crisp wings tossed in butter and lemon pepper, ordered "wet" if you want them glossy. Rappers name-check it; locals argue about it.

    Try it at J.R. Crickets (Midtown) · American Deli (all over town)

  2. No. 2

    Fried chicken & Southern sides

    Collards, mac and cheese baked till the edges crackle, candied yams, cornbread. A meat-and-three plate is a Sunday ritual here.

    Try it at Mary Mac's Tea Room (Ponce de Leon Ave) · Busy Bee Cafe (Westside)

  3. No. 3

    Peach cobbler

    Georgia is the Peach State for a reason. Warm, syrupy fruit under a buttery crust, best with a scoop of vanilla melting on top.

    Try it at Paschal's (Castleberry Hill) · Busy Bee Cafe

03

Culture

How the city sounds, where it hangs out, and when it throws a party.

Music

Atlanta shaped modern hip-hop: OutKast, T.I., Ludacris, and the trap sound that took over the charts all came from here. The roots run deeper too, through gospel, R&B, and the Fox Theatre stage.

  • Catch a show at the Tabernacle or Variety Playhouse
  • Smith's Olde Bar for up-and-coming bands
  • The Fox Theatre, a 1929 movie palace, for Broadway tours

Neighborhoods

Little Five Points
Vintage shops, record stores, and the city's weird heart.
Inman Park
Victorian homes and BeltLine patios.
West End
Historic Black neighborhood with great food and the Wren's Nest.
Decatur
A walkable square of bars and bookshops, a train ride east.

Events

  • AprAtlanta Dogwood Festival in Piedmont Park

04

Visitor Tips

The stuff I'd text you before your flight lands.

Getting around

  • MARTA trains run straight from the airport to Downtown, Midtown, and Decatur. A one-way ride is $2.50.
  • Walk or rent a scooter along the BeltLine between neighborhoods.
  • Traffic is real. Avoid I-75/85 "the Connector" from 4 to 7 p.m.
  • Peachtree Street is not one road. There are dozens. Check the full address.

Best time to visit

  • Spring (Mar–May): dogwoods and azaleas bloom, mild days. My pick.
  • Fall (Sep–Nov): cooler air, festivals, football.
  • Summer: hot and humid, with afternoon storms. Plan indoor afternoons.
  • Watch for pollen season in late March if you have allergies.

Money-saving

  • The CityPASS bundles the Aquarium, Coca-Cola, and more at a discount.
  • The MLK park, BeltLine, Piedmont Park, and many festivals are free.
  • Skip the rental car if you're staying intown; MARTA plus walking covers a lot.
  • Ponce City Market and Krog Street Market food halls let you eat well for under $20.
